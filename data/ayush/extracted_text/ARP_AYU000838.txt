Background :  Argyreia speciosa roots are widely used in Indian traditional system of medicine for the treatment of obesity. The present study was designed to evaluate the antiobesity effects of ethanolic extract of Argyreia speciosa roots in rats fed with atherogenic diet. Obesity was induced in albino rats by feeding atherogenic diet daily for 42 days in addition to normal diet. Treatment was continued for 6 weeks. Body weight was measured initially and then every week thereafter. Food intake was measured during last five consecutive days. On day 42, serum lipid levels were estimated and then the animals were killed by an overdose of ether, liver and parametrial adipose tissues were removed and weighed immediately. The liver triglyceride content was estimated. The influence of ethanolic extract of Argyreia speciosa on pancreatic lipase activity was also determined. Body weight at 2-6 weeks and the final parametrial adipose tissue weights were significantly lowered in rats treated with Argyreia speciosa extract 500 mg/kg/day as compared with atherogenic diet control rats. The extract also significantly reduced the serum levels of total cholesterol (TC\ LDL\ TG\ alone. In addition, the extract inhibited the induction of fatty liver with accumulation of hepatic triglycerides. The extract also showed significant inhibition of pancreatic lipase activity. In conclusion, Argyreia speciosa roots extract produced antiobesity effects in atherogenic diet induced obese rats, which might be due to inhibition of lipid absorption from small intestine through the inhibition of pancreatic lipase activity.
